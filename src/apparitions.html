Where private revelation stands

Public revelation ended with the apostles. Apparitions and messages, even those the Church approves, belong to private revelation. “It is not their role to improve or complete Christ's definitive Revelation, but to help live more fully by it in a certain period of history” (Catechism 67). A Catholic is free to accept an approved apparition with prudent devotion, and is never bound to believe it as part of the faith.

How the Church judges today

On May 17, 2024 the Dicastery for the Doctrine of the Faith published new Norms for Proceeding in the Discernment of Alleged Supernatural Phenomena, in force from May 19, 2024. As a rule the Church no longer declares that an apparition is supernatural. She gives one of six judgments:

JudgmentWhat it means
Nihil obstatNo objection. Signs of the Holy Spirit's action are present; the bishop may encourage the devotion, and the faithful may adhere to it prudently.
Prae oculis habeaturPositive signs, but some elements need clarification and discernment; caution is asked.
CuraturCritical elements are present, but the devotion is widespread and bearing fruit; it is not encouraged, nor forbidden, and is watched over.
Sub mandatoProblems arise less from the phenomenon than from people or groups misusing it; the Holy See entrusts its pastoral care to the bishop or a delegate.
Prohibetur et obstruaturSerious risks outweigh any good; the bishop declares that adherence is not permitted.
Declaratio de non supernaturalitateThe bishop, with the Dicastery's approval, declares the phenomenon not supernatural, on concrete evidence.

Older apparitions were recognized under earlier procedures, usually by the local bishop declaring them worthy of belief. Those recognitions stand, and each page here gives the date and authority.
