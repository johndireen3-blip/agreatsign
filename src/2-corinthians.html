Prophecy in 2 Corinthians

This is Paul's most personal letter, written out of weakness and affliction. Its hope is a transformation already under way: a veil lifted, an inner self renewed day by day, and a dwelling from God waiting in heaven.

The book at a glance

Paul wrote again to Corinth, probably from Macedonia, after a painful visit and a severe letter. He defends his ministry against rivals, speaks openly of his sufferings, and organizes the collection for the poor church in Jerusalem. Many scholars think the letter as we have it gathers more than one of Paul's letters to Corinth.

2 Corinthians has few predictions. It looks forward through the unfolding work of grace in the Church now, and through two images that run from Genesis to Revelation: the temple where God dwells and the bride betrothed to Christ.

The passages

2 Corinthians 3:12–18ImageUnfolding
“All of us, gazing with unveiled face on the glory of the Lord, are being transformed into the same image from glory to glory, as from the Lord who is the Spirit.”
Reading
Moses veiled his shining face after speaking with God (Exodus 34:33–35). Paul takes the veil as an image of reading the old covenant without Christ, “but whenever a person turns to the Lord the veil is removed” (3:16). Then the face of every believer becomes a mirror of the Lord's glory.
Why unfolding
The verb is present and continuous: “are being transformed.” The change happens now, gradually, in the life of the Church; its completion is the seeing “face to face” of 1 Corinthians 13:12.
2 Corinthians 4:16–5:10ProphecyFuture
“For we know that if our earthly dwelling, a tent, should be destroyed, we have a building from God, a dwelling not made with hands, eternal in heaven.”
Reading
“Although our outer self is wasting away, our inner self is being renewed day by day” (4:16), and “this momentary light affliction is producing for us an eternal weight of glory” (4:17). Paul would rather “leave the body and go home to the Lord” (5:8), a verse the Catechism cites on the reward given soon after death (CCC 1021). Then he adds: “For we must all appear before the judgment seat of Christ” (5:10).
Why future
Both the particular judgment at death and the Last Judgment at Christ's return are still ahead for each person (CCC 1021–1022, 1038–1041).
For now
Paul's answer to fear is courage: “we look not to what is seen but to what is unseen” (4:18).
2 Corinthians 6:16ImageUnfolding
“For we are the temple of the living God; as God said: ‘I will live with them and move among them, and I will be their God and they shall be my people.’”
Reading
Paul joins the promises of the prophets (Leviticus 26:12; Ezekiel 37:27) and says they are kept now, in the community of believers. The Catechism cites this verse when it calls the Church the temple of the Holy Spirit (CCC 797).
Why unfolding
The temple is still being built (Ephesians 2:21–22). Its completion is the new Jerusalem, where the same words are heard again: “Behold, God's dwelling is with the human race” (Revelation 21:3).

The Temple of His Body.
2 Corinthians 11:2–3ImageUnfoldingFuture
“…I betrothed you to one husband to present you as a chaste virgin to Christ.”
Reading
Paul speaks as the friend who arranged a marriage. The Church is betrothed now and will be presented to Christ at the end; the Catechism uses this verse when it speaks of the Church as the Bride of Christ (CCC 796). Then he adds a warning drawn from Genesis 3: “as the serpent deceived Eve by his cunning, your thoughts may be corrupted” (11:3).
Why two labels
The betrothal is the life of the Church now, with its trials and temptations. The wedding is still to come: “the wedding day of the Lamb” and his bride (Revelation 19:7).
See also
Prophecy in Genesis; Revelation 12, where the woman and the serpent meet again.
