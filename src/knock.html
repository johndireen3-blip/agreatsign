Pillar II · Apparitions · Ireland, 1879

Our Lady of Knock

On a wet August evening in 1879, in a small village in County Mayo, fifteen people saw a vision at the gable wall of their parish church: Our Lady, St. Joseph and St. John the Evangelist, and beside them an altar with a Lamb standing before a cross, surrounded by angels. For about two hours they watched and prayed in the rain. No word was spoken.

At a glance

PlaceThe gable wall of the parish church of Knock, County Mayo, in the Archdiocese of Tuam, Ireland
DatesA single apparition, on the evening of Thursday, August 21, 1879. The Shrine puts its length at about two hours; some later accounts say nearly three.
SeersFifteen people of the village, whose testimony was taken by the commission of 1879. They ranged from a boy of about five, John Curry, to an elderly woman, Bridget Trench. Among them were Mary Byrne (also spelled Beirne), who was among the first to see the figures, and Patrick Hill, 11, who gave the fullest description.
Church judgmentNo bishop ever issued a formal decree declaring the apparition supernatural. Archbishop John MacHale of Tuam set up a commission of inquiry in October 1879, which judged the witnesses' testimony “trustworthy and satisfactory”; the Archbishop made no public statement. A second commission, set up by Archbishop Thomas Gilmartin of Tuam, took sworn evidence from the last surviving witnesses in 1935–36 and found their testimony “satisfactory.” The popes have since honored the shrine: St. John Paul II came as a pilgrim for the centenary on September 30, 1979, named its new church a basilica and gave it a Golden Rose, and Pope Francis raised it to an international shrine on March 19, 2021. The details are below. Recognized in practice long before the 2024 norms; there is no judgment under them.
FeastAugust 17, Our Lady of Knock, in the calendar of Ireland (not in the universal calendar). The anniversary on August 21 is kept at the Shrine, and a public novena is held there every August.
PilgrimageKnock Shrine, since 2021 an International Shrine of Special Eucharistic and Marian Devotion, with the Apparition Chapel at the old gable and the Basilica of Our Lady, Queen of Ireland
WeightPrivate revelation, long accepted and honored by the Church: Catholics may accept it with prudent devotion but are not bound to believe it (CCC 67).

What happened

On the evening of August 21, 1879, before dark, some of the villagers passing the church saw a bright light at the gable and figures standing in it. Word went round, and people came and went over the next two hours, some kneeling in the rain to pray the Rosary. Fifteen of them later gave testimony.

The fullest account comes from Patrick Hill, an eleven-year-old boy, in his deposition to the commission of October 8, 1879, now published by the Shrine. In the center stood the Blessed Virgin, in white robes, wearing “a brilliant crown on her head, and over the forehead where the crown filled the brow, a beautiful rose.” “Her hands were raised to the height of the shoulders, as if in prayer, with the palms facing one another,” and her eyes were turned toward heaven. St. Joseph stood at her right hand, his head bent toward her, his hands joined. Beside them was St. John the Evangelist: “St. John was dressed like a bishop preaching,” with a small miter, holding a book in his left hand and raising his right.

Beyond them stood a plain altar, and on it a lamb, the size of a lamb eight weeks old, with a large cross standing upright behind it. Angels hovered around the lamb; the boy saw their wings but not their faces. The figures were “full and round as if they had a body and life,” he said, but they “said nothing.” It rained the whole time and Patrick was soaked, yet “the rain did not wet the figures which appeared before me.”

The accounts above follow the depositions as published by Knock Shrine. They are what the witnesses reported, which the Church weighed; they are not doctrine.

What Our Lady asked

At Knock Our Lady asked nothing in words. That is what makes it unlike every other apparition on these pages, and it is itself the message. Pope Francis put it simply in 2021: “You know that in the apparition of Knock the Virgin does not say a word.” And then: “But even her silence is a language; indeed, it is the most expressive language that is given to us.”

Be silent before the mystery

“The message from Knock is the great value of silence for faith,” Pope Francis said. The witnesses did not question the figures. They looked, and they prayed.

Look to the Lamb

Mary, Joseph and John do not stand at the center for their own sake. The scene is drawn toward the altar, the Lamb and the cross: toward Christ, sacrificed and living, present in the Eucharist.

Pray together

The first response at Knock was the Rosary, prayed by poor villagers kneeling in the rain at the church wall. The Shrine has kept that response ever since.

Hope

Nothing at Knock threatens or warns. Heaven came close to a poor village, and its people were given something to see, not something to fear.

The Lamb on the altar

What sets Knock apart is the altar. Other apparitions show Our Lady alone or with her Son; at Knock she stands beside the altar of the Lamb, as she stood beside the cross. St. John Paul II came to it as a pilgrim in 1979, and in 2021 the Holy See named Knock a shrine of “special Eucharistic and Marian devotion,” because the two belong together there.

The figures and their places
Our Lady
Crowned, in white, her hands raised in prayer and her eyes on heaven. She does not point to herself.
St. Joseph
Bowed toward her, his hands joined: the guardian of the Holy Family, honoring his wife and the Lord she bore.
St. John
Vested like a bishop, a book in his hand: the beloved disciple to whom Jesus entrusted his mother at the cross (John 19:26–27), and in the Church's tradition the John of the Book of Revelation.
The Lamb and the cross
On a plain altar, with angels around it: the Lamb of God, who takes away the sin of the world (John 1:29), sacrificed on the cross and now living.

How the Church received Knock

Knock is often listed among the “approved” apparitions. That is fair, but it should be understood exactly: no decree ever declared it supernatural. The Church's acceptance of Knock was built up over more than a century, act by act.

DateAct
October 1879Archbishop John MacHale of Tuam appoints a commission of three priests: Archdeacon Bartholomew Cavanagh, parish priest of Knock, Canon James Waldron and Canon Ulick Bourke. It examines fifteen witnesses and reports that their testimony, taken as a whole, is “trustworthy and satisfactory.” The Archbishop makes no public statement.
1935–1936Archbishop Thomas Gilmartin of Tuam sets up a second commission, which takes evidence under oath from August 24, 1935 to April 14, 1936. It examines the two surviving witnesses at Knock, Mary O'Connell (née Byrne), then 86, and Patrick Byrne, 71. In her sworn statement of January 27, 1936, Mary Byrne declared: “I am quite clear about everything I have said, I make this statement knowing I am going before my God.” The judges find the witnesses “upright” and their testimony “satisfactory.”
September 30, 1979St. John Paul II comes to Knock for the centenary: “Here I am at the goal of my journey to Ireland: the Shrine of Our Lady at Knock.” He announces that the new church will be known as the “Basilica of Our Lady, Queen of Ireland,” and offers the shrine “a Rose in gold,” the Golden Rose, as his “testimony of gratitude to Mary.”
August 26, 2018Pope Francis visits Knock during the World Meeting of Families, prays at the Apparition Chapel, gives the shrine a golden rosary and prays the Angelus there.
September 1, 2019Bishop Francis Duffy of Ardagh and Clonmacnois recognizes the healing of Marion Carroll, who was cured at Knock in 1989 during a blessing with the Blessed Sacrament: “I recognise that Marion was healed from her long-standing illness while on pilgrimage in this sacred place.” It is the first cure the Church in Ireland has recognized as miraculous.
March 19, 2021On the solemnity of St. Joseph, Pope Francis, by video message, marks the elevation of the national shrine to an “International Shrine of Special Eucharistic and Marian Devotion.” The designation had been announced by the Irish bishops on March 11, 2021.

Where things stand. Knock rests on two diocesan commissions that found its witnesses credible, and on more than a century of the Church's public honor, including the visits and gifts of two popes. It has never received a formal declaration of supernatural character, and under the 2024 norms none is needed for the devotion to continue. Catholics may venerate Our Lady of Knock with full confidence in the Church's blessing on the shrine, without claiming for the apparition a judgment the Church has not given.

Knock and the Book of Revelation

Knock does not interpret the Book of Revelation. But no other apparition looks so much like one of its pages.

  • The Lamb that was slain. “Then I saw standing in the midst of the throne and the four living creatures and the elders, a Lamb that seemed to have been slain” (Revelation 5:6). Around him the angels cry out: “Worthy is the Lamb that was slain to receive power and riches, wisdom and strength, honor and glory and blessing” (Revelation 5:12). The Catechism reads this vision as the heavenly liturgy, where the Lamb is “Christ crucified and risen, the one high priest of the true sanctuary” (CCC 1137). At Knock the angels hover around the Lamb on the altar.
  • Those who follow the Lamb. “Then I looked and there was the Lamb standing on Mount Zion” (Revelation 14:1), with the redeemed around him: “these are the ones who follow the Lamb wherever he goes” (Revelation 14:4). Mary, Joseph and John stand turned toward the Lamb, as the saints do in heaven.
  • The heavenly liturgy. The Catechism names among those who share in that liturgy “the heavenly powers” and “the all-holy Mother of God (the Woman)” (CCC 1138). Every Mass joins it, and the invitation to Communion recalls the angel's words: “Blessed are those who have been called to the wedding feast of the Lamb” (Revelation 19:9).
  • The altar and the prayers of the saints. “Another angel came and stood at the altar, holding a gold censer” with “the prayers of all the holy ones” (Revelation 8:3). The villagers of Knock knelt in the rain and prayed before an altar seen in light.
  • Silence in heaven. “When he broke open the seventh seal, there was silence in heaven for about half an hour” (Revelation 8:1). Revelation, too, knows a holy silence before the mystery of God.
  • The woman and the beloved disciple. At the cross Jesus said to his mother, “Woman, behold, your son,” and to the disciple, “Behold, your mother” (John 19:26–27). At Knock the same two stand together beside the Lamb, and the mother is crowned, like the woman of Revelation 12:1.
Read with careBecause Our Lady said nothing at Knock, words are sometimes put in her mouth: “messages” of Knock that circulate online, or readings of the vision as a sign of coming events. None of them comes from the witnesses or the Church. The witnesses saw, prayed and testified, and the Church has kept to what they said. Knock adds nothing to the faith (CCC 67); it points to what the Church already has at every altar.

For reflection

Can I be silent before the Lamb?

The people of Knock did not ask for a message. They knelt and looked. The next time I am before the altar or the tabernacle, could I spend a few minutes saying nothing, and simply look at the Lamb of God?
