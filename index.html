Four ways a prophecy can stand

Every prophetic passage on this site carries one of four labels. They are a reading aid, not a timetable: many prophecies are fulfilled in more than one way, and the Church never sets dates for the end (Matthew 24:36; Catechism 673).

Fulfilled

Accomplished in history, above all in Christ. Micah 5:1, the ruler from Bethlehem.

Unfolding

Being worked out now in the life of the Church. Matthew 24:14, the Gospel preached to all nations.

Future

Clearly still to come at Christ's return. 1 Thessalonians 4:16–17; Revelation 21:1.

Mystery

Its meaning or moment is not yet clear, and the Church has not settled it. Revelation 11:3, the two witnesses.

Prophecy and apocalypse are not the same thing: one is God's word to his people in their own day, the other an unveiling of history from heaven's side. Why the difference matters.

Why read Revelation at all?

People often set Revelation aside for one of two reasons: either “it has all happened already,” or “it is too hard to understand.” Both miss what the book offers. Christ's victory is won, but his Body, the Church, is still being built, and Revelation shows us our part in it. Read Fr. John's answer to both.

A word on private revelationApparitions and private revelations, even those the Church approves, do not add to the faith. They help us “live more fully by” the revelation of Christ in a certain period of history (Catechism 67). This site keeps them in that place.
