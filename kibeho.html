Pillar II · Apparitions · Rwanda, 1981

Our Lady of Kibeho

In 1981, at a school in the hills of southern Rwanda, a young student said the Mother of God had come to her as Nyina wa Jambo, “Mother of the Word.” Two other students followed. She called her children to conversion while there was still time, and she wept. Twelve years later Rwanda suffered genocide.

At a glance

PlaceKibeho, in southern Rwanda, then in the Diocese of Butare and since 1992 in the Diocese of Gikongoro
DatesFrom November 28, 1981. Alphonsine's apparitions continued until November 28, 1989; Nathalie's from January 12, 1982 to December 3, 1983; Marie-Claire's from March 2 to September 15, 1982. The bishop's declaration speaks of the principal period of apparitions as ending in 1983.
Seers recognizedThree students of the secondary school at Kibeho: Alphonsine Mumureke, Nathalie Mukamazimpaka and Marie-Claire Mukangango. Others also claimed visions in the years that followed; the bishop judged that “only the three initial testimonies merit being considered authentic.”
Church judgmentDeclared authentic by the Bishop of Gikongoro, Augustin Misago, on June 29, 2001, the Solemnity of Sts. Peter and Paul, during Mass in his cathedral: “Yes, the Virgin Mary appeared at Kibeho on November 28th, 1981 and in the months that followed.” The Holy See Press Office published his declaration the same day. Earlier, on August 15, 1988, the Bishop of Butare, Jean-Baptiste Gahamanyi, had authorized public devotion at Kibeho, without yet judging the apparitions. The recognition was given before the 2024 norms and stands. It is often described as the first Church-recognized Marian apparition in Africa.
FeastNovember 28, Our Lady of Kibeho, as set by the bishop's declaration (not in the universal calendar)
PilgrimageThe Shrine of Our Lady of Sorrows at Kibeho, whose first stone was laid on November 28, 1992
WeightPrivate revelation, approved: Catholics may accept it with prudent devotion but are not bound to believe it (CCC 67).

What happened

On November 28, 1981, Alphonsine Mumureke, a student at the school in Kibeho, said she saw a woman of great beauty. Asked who she was, the woman answered that she was Nyina wa Jambo, “Mother of the Word,” which, as the bishop's declaration explains, she herself said means Mother of God. At first she was met with doubt. In January 1982 Nathalie Mukamazimpaka began to receive apparitions, and in March Marie-Claire Mukangango.

As word spread, the apparitions drew great crowds to Kibeho. The diocese set up two commissions in 1982, one medical and one theological. After almost twenty years of study, through war and genocide, Bishop Misago gave his judgment. He pointed to the seers' sound mental health and sincerity, “the absence of sensationalism” in their behavior, and a message “consistent, relevant, and orthodox,” in conformity with Scripture and the living Tradition of the Church.

What Our Lady asked

The bishop's declaration summarizes the message under ten headings. The quotations here are from the English translation of the declaration given by EWTN.

“Repent, repent, repent!” … “Convert while there is still time.”

Conversion of heart

The first and most urgent appeal: to turn from sin and return to God, now, while there is time.

Pray always, with an undivided heart

Prayer that is sincere and constant, not occasional or for show (cf. 1 Thessalonians 5:17).

The Rosary

Marian devotion expressed through sincere and regular praying of the Rosary, and the renewal of the Rosary of the Seven Sorrows.

Suffering that saves

Suffering accepted and offered, united with Christ, can bear fruit for others. The seers themselves offered prayer and, in Nathalie's case, long fasts.

Mary's sorrow

The deep sorrow of the Mother of God over the moral state of the world, which, the message warned, was on the edge of the abyss.

Pray for the Church

To pray always for the Church, “when many troubles are upon it in the times to come,” and to build a chapel as a sign of remembrance of her visits.

The visions of August 15, 1982

On the Solemnity of the Assumption, 1982, before a large crowd, the seers wept. As they and witnesses later reported, they were shown terrifying scenes: a river of blood, people killing one another, bodies left unburied, a tree in flames, a gaping abyss. The bishop's declaration describes them simply as “the frightening visions of August 15th, 1982,” and notes that the seers saw the Virgin crying.

Less than twelve years later, from April to July 1994, the genocide against the Tutsi took the lives of hundreds of thousands of people, by most estimates between 800,000 and a million, in about one hundred days. Hutu who refused to kill, or who tried to protect their neighbors, were murdered as well. Kibeho itself was not spared: people who had sought refuge in its parish church were massacred in April 1994, and the school where the apparitions took place was also struck. Marie-Claire Mukangango, one of the three seers, was among those lost in the violence of those years; accounts differ on the circumstances of her death. In 1995, Kibeho was again the scene of killings, in a camp for displaced people.

What the Church has saidBishop's declaration
The declaration
Bishop Misago listed among the signs of credibility that the visions of August 15, 1982 “proved to be prophetic due to the human dramas in Rwanda and throughout the country of the Great Lake region in recent years.” Elsewhere he wrote more tentatively: “it strikes us that the great misfortune that struck Rwanda” in the past decade “might have been, in one way or another, predicted” through the apparitions.
The caution
He also insisted that the relapse of the “converted” into sin, the horrors of war and the terror of the genocide “cannot be taken as a sufficient argument against the credibility” of the apparitions. In other words, the warning was given and not heeded. He warned pilgrims against “a certain magical mentality,” and against rumors of miracles and new visionaries.
How to read it
The visions are not a key for explaining the genocide, still less for assigning blame by people or by ethnic group, and they take nothing away from the responsibility of those who planned and carried out the killings. If they were a warning, they were the warning of a mother who saw what hatred would do to her children and wept for all of them.

On this ground, speech should give way to silence and prayer. The victims of 1994 were real men, women and children, many of them killed by neighbors, some in churches where they had fled for safety. Kibeho is today a place where Rwandans pray for the dead, for survivors who still carry the wounds, and for the long work of forgiveness and reconciliation.

The Rosary of the Seven Sorrows

Marie-Claire Mukangango said that Our Lady entrusted her with making known an old prayer, the Rosary (or chaplet) of the Seven Sorrows, which belongs to the tradition of the Servite Order. The bishop wrote that this prayer “deserves to be renewed and spread in the Church,” and added at once: “However, this prayer does not replace the Holy Rosary.”

For each sorrow one meditates on the scene and prays an Our Father and seven Hail Marys:

  1. The prophecy of Simeon: “and you yourself a sword will pierce” (Luke 2:34–35).
  2. The flight into Egypt (Matthew 2:13–15).
  3. The loss of the Child Jesus in the Temple (Luke 2:41–50).
  4. Mary meets Jesus on the way to Calvary (cf. Luke 23:26–31).
  5. Jesus dies on the Cross: “Standing by the cross of Jesus were his mother…” (John 19:25–30).
  6. Jesus is taken down from the Cross (John 19:38–40).
  7. Jesus is laid in the tomb (John 19:41–42).

The Seven Sorrows are not a devotion to sadness. They teach us to stand with Mary beside those who suffer, as she stood by the Cross, and to bring our own griefs to her who knows them. After 1994 that is what many in Rwanda did.

Kibeho and the Book of Revelation

Kibeho does not interpret the Book of Revelation, but its images echo it.

  • The Mother of the Word. “In the beginning was the Word… And the Word became flesh” (John 1:1, 14). The woman of Revelation 12:1 is the Mother of the child who is that Word.
  • A mother in anguish. The woman “wailed aloud in pain as she labored to give birth” (Revelation 12:2). At Kibeho the seers saw the Mother weeping.
  • The dragon's war. The dragon, enraged, goes off “to wage war against the rest of her offspring” (Revelation 12:17). Revelation does not hide the reality of murderous evil in history, and neither does Kibeho.
  • The cry of the slain. John saw under the altar “the souls of those who had been slaughtered,” crying out “How long will it be, holy and true master” (Revelation 6:9–10). The Church remembers every victim before God.
  • Every tear. Those who come through the great distress will be shepherded by the Lamb, “and God will wipe away every tear from their eyes” (Revelation 7:17). This is the hope Kibeho holds out to Rwanda.
Read with careKibeho is sometimes told as a sensational story of prophecy fulfilled. The Church is more sober: she recognized three seers and not the others, warned against a magical mentality and rumors of new visions, and placed the message where it belongs, in a call to conversion, prayer and the Rosary. The visions of 1982 set no dates and name no peoples. Private revelation never adds to the faith (CCC 67).

For reflection

Convert while there is still time

Is there a resentment I am nursing, a person I refuse to forgive, a neighbor I have written off? Kibeho shows where hatred ends. Mary asks for conversion of heart now, beginning with me.
