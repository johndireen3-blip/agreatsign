St. Carlo Acutis and the Wedding Feast of the Lamb

A teenager from Milan in jeans and sneakers, good with computers, who went to Mass every day and died of leukemia at fifteen. Carlo Acutis wrote no commentary on Revelation and taught nothing about the end times. He is here because he lived for what stands at the center of that book: the Lamb who was slain, and the feast to which the Lamb calls us. He called the Eucharist “my highway to heaven.”

At a glance

WhoCarlo Acutis (1991–2006), born in London on May 3, 1991, to Italian parents and raised in Milan; died at San Gerardo Hospital in Monza on October 12, 2006, aged fifteen, a few days after he was diagnosed with leukemia
StandingSaint declared Venerable on July 5, 2018; beatified in the Basilica of St. Francis in Assisi on October 10, 2020, when Cardinal Agostino Vallini, the papal legate, read Pope Francis's apostolic letter; canonized by Pope Leo XIV in St. Peter's Square on September 7, 2025, together with St. Pier Giorgio Frassati. Feast: October 12.
Where he restsAssisi, in the Sanctuary of the Spoliation (the Church of Santa Maria Maggiore), where St. Francis stripped himself of his clothes before the bishop and gave himself wholly to God. Carlo's body was moved there in April 2019. It is not incorrupt: the Bishop of Assisi said it was in the normal state of a body after death, and a silicone likeness covers the face.
The workHis exhibition of Eucharistic miracles, a website and printed panels he put together as a teenager; a second exhibition on Marian apparitions, begun in 2006 and finished after his death; short sayings and notes, published by his family and quoted by the popes
WeightThe Church holds up his holiness, not a teaching. His sayings are a young man's spiritual maxims, and the ones quoted here come from papal texts and official sources. He made no prophecies about the end of the world, and the Church attributes none to him.

Why he is here

To be plain from the start: Carlo Acutis left no teaching on the end times. He did not comment on Revelation, name an Antichrist or set a date. Anything online that says otherwise has no reliable source (see the caution below). He belongs among these witnesses for two other reasons.

A witness to the Lamb. The worship of heaven in Revelation is centered on a Lamb. The angels, the living creatures and the elders cry out, “Worthy is the Lamb that was slain to receive power and riches, wisdom and strength, honor and glory and blessing” (Revelation 5:12). The book reaches its climax in a wedding: “Then the angel said to me, ‘Write this: Blessed are those who have been called to the wedding feast of the Lamb’” (Revelation 19:9). The Church hears that beatitude at every Mass and calls the Lord's Supper an anticipation of “the wedding feast of the Lamb in the heavenly Jerusalem” (CCC 1329). In the Eucharist she receives “a pledge of the life to come” (CCC 1402–1405). Carlo built his short life on exactly this, the Lamb's feast tasted in advance, morning by morning. The liturgy of heaven that John saw was, for him, the Mass in his parish church.

A saint who used the web to point to Christ. Pope Francis held him up to young people in Christus Vivit (2019): the digital world can expose young people to “self-absorption, isolation and empty pleasure,” and Carlo was well aware of its pull, “yet he knew how to use the new communications technology to transmit the Gospel, to communicate values and beauty” (Christus Vivit 104–106). His best-known work, the exhibition of Eucharistic miracles, began as a website. A site like this one, built to lead people to Christ through the Book of Revelation, owes him something.

Patron of this projectThe Ephesus Project, the family of websites this site belongs to, takes St. Carlo Acutis as its principal patron, under the protection of Our Lady, the Woman clothed with the sun (Revelation 12:1). Its co-patrons are St. Bernard of Clairvaux, St. Teresa Benedicta of the Cross (Edith Stein), St. Rose of Lima and Pope St. John Paul II. The project was dedicated on August 22, 2023, the Memorial of the Queenship of Mary.

His life briefly

Carlo's parents were not especially devout, but he was drawn to God as a small child. He made his First Communion at seven and from then on rarely missed daily Mass, often staying to pray before the tabernacle. He prayed the Rosary every day, spent time in Eucharistic adoration, and taught catechism. He was also an ordinary boy of his generation, with a gift for computers that he put at the service of his faith.

In early October 2006 he fell ill. It was a fast-moving leukemia. He offered his sufferings, in the words Cardinal Becciu, then Prefect of the Congregation for the Causes of Saints, quoted from him: “I want to offer all my sufferings for the Lord, for the Pope, and for the Church” (Vatican News, June 17, 2020). He died on October 12, 2006. At his own wish he was buried in Assisi, the town of St. Francis that he loved.

The Church recognized two healings through his intercession: a Brazilian boy born with a defect of the pancreas, for the beatification, and Valeria Valverde, a young woman from Costa Rica who recovered after a bicycle accident in Florence in 2022, for the canonization (approved by Pope Francis on May 23, 2024; EWTN News). The canonization was first set for April 2025 and was postponed when Pope Francis died. Pope Leo XIV celebrated it that September.

The Eucharistic miracles exhibition

International Exhibition of Eucharistic Miracles · begun 2002His workUnfolding
What it is
A catalogue, in photographs and short texts, of Eucharistic miracles that the Church has recognized in many countries across the centuries: events reported where a Host bled, or was preserved, or was seen by many as flesh. Carlo had the idea after visiting an exhibition at the Rimini Meeting in 2002 and worked on it, with his family's help, for about two and a half years. The set offered for download on its official site has 166 posters (miracolieucaristici.org). Cardinal Angelo Comastri and Msgr. Raffaello Martinelli, then of the Congregation for the Doctrine of the Faith, wrote prefaces for the catalogue.
Where it has gone
According to its official site it has been shown on all five continents. In the United States alone it has been hosted in thousands of parishes and more than a hundred universities. It has also been shown in China and Indonesia and at the Shrine of Our Lady of Fatima, and the bishops' conferences of the Philippines, Argentina and Vietnam, among others, have promoted it (miracolieucaristici.org). Parishes can download the panels free of charge in several languages from the official exhibition site; in Italy they can also be requested from the Associazione Amici di Carlo Acutis (carloacutis.com).
Why he made it
Not to collect wonders. His mother recalls him saying that if people understood the importance of the Eucharist, the churches would be so crowded that no one could get in (Avvenire, 2020). He hoped the signs would lead them to the One who is truly present on every altar. Since Christ is present in the churches near our homes, he said, “Jerusalem is right on our doorstep” (official exhibition site).
Read with care
Recognized miracles are signs for faith, judged by the Church where they happened. Catholics are not bound to believe any particular one (compare CCC 67). The Real Presence does not depend on them; it rests on Christ's own word. “I am the living bread that came down from heaven; whoever eats this bread will live forever” (John 6:51).

What he said

Only sayings with a reliable source are given here, and for each the best source we could check. Several come from Pope Leo XIV's homily at his canonization, which quotes Carlo again and again. English wording varies because these are translations from Italian.

SayingWhere it is found
“The Eucharist is my highway to heaven.”Quoted by Cardinal Agostino Vallini, papal legate, in his homily at the beatification, Assisi, October 10, 2020; the words were engraved on the reliquary of Carlo's heart (Catholic World Report; CNS via OSV). Also the title of an early biography, by Nicola Gori, later postulator of his cause: Eucaristia. La mia autostrada per il cielo (San Paolo, 2007).
“To always be close to Jesus, that's my life plan.”Written when he was seven, as given by the Associazione Amici di Carlo Acutis (carloacutis.com); the same site also translates it “To be always united to Jesus, this is my program for life.” Italian: Essere sempre unito a Gesù, ecco il mio programma di vita.
“Everyone is born as an original, but many people end up dying as photocopies.”Pope Francis, Christus Vivit 106 (2019). The image is older than Carlo (the English poet Edward Young asked in 1759 why, “Born Originals,” we “die Copies”), but Carlo made it his own, and the Pope quotes it as his.
“In front of the sun, you get a tan. In front of the Eucharist, you become a saint!”Pope Leo XIV, homily at the canonization, September 7, 2025
“Sadness is looking at yourself; happiness is looking at God.”Pope Leo XIV, canonization homily
“Conversion is nothing more than shifting your gaze from below to above; a simple movement of the eyes is enough.”Pope Leo XIV, canonization homily
“The only thing we really have to fear is sin.”Pope Leo XIV, canonization homily (“Carlo wrote”)
People “are so concerned with the beauty of their bodies and do not care about the beauty of their souls.”Pope Leo XIV, canonization homily (“in his own words”)
“Not I, but God.”Pope Leo XIV, canonization homily (“as Carlo used to say”); the official site gives it as “Not me, but God.” His notes were published under this title by his mother and Nicola Gori, Non io ma Dio (Piemme).

Other sayings often quoted, such as “The more often we receive the Eucharist, the more we become like Jesus” and “Our goal must be the infinite, not the finite,” are probably his and appear in books published with his family, but we could not trace them to a page or an official text, so they are left out here.

A caution about sayings onlineAround his canonization, videos and posts have appeared, some narrated by a voice presented as his mother (“my son Carlo”), that attribute to him words about the Antichrist, a secret note of warnings for the Church, or the “Three Days of Darkness,” or that tie him to a coming worldwide “Warning.” None of them gives a source that can be checked: no book, letter, interview or date. Nothing like them appears in his published notes, on the official site, in anything from his cause, or in any papal text. Some channels of this kind admit in their own fine print that their “testimonies” are fictional (Avvenire, April 2026). The line “Tell everyone that God is making haste,” sometimes tied to him, comes from messages attributed to another seer, not from Carlo. His postulation and the official association have asked his devotees “to abstain from spreading unfounded news” (carloacutis.com). The Diocese of Assisi has also denounced the online sale of supposed relics of Carlo (NCR, April 2025). A saint who loved the truth deserves to be quoted truthfully. If a saying cannot be traced, don't share it.

How to live it

  • Go to Mass, and more than on Sundays if you can. The Eucharist is “the source and summit of the Christian life” (CCC 1324). “Whoever eats my flesh and drinks my blood has eternal life, and I will raise him on the last day” (John 6:54). For Carlo the shortest road to the wedding feast of the Lamb ran through the altar of his parish.
  • Stay a while before the tabernacle. A visit to the Blessed Sacrament is “a proof of gratitude, an expression of love, and a duty of adoration toward Christ our Lord” (CCC 1418; see 1378). Carlo's “sun” was the Host. Ten minutes in its light is a beginning.
  • Pray the Rosary. He prayed it every day. The Church calls it an “epitome of the whole Gospel” (CCC 971).
  • Go to confession. “The only thing we really have to fear is sin.” Confession is the way back to freedom, not a weight to carry.
  • Love the poor. “The Eucharist commits us to the poor” (CCC 1397). Of Carlo and Pier Giorgio together, Pope Leo XIV said that “they practiced charity generously.” The Lamb's feast is not something to keep for yourself.
  • Use the screen for good. Carlo used the internet to lead people to Christ. What would it look like to do the same with the phone in your pocket?

For reflection

Original or photocopy?

“Blessed are those who have been called to the wedding feast of the Lamb” (Revelation 19:9). Carlo heard that call at every Mass and answered it with an ordinary teenage life. Where am I copying what everyone else wants, and what would it mean to let Jesus make me the original he had in mind?
